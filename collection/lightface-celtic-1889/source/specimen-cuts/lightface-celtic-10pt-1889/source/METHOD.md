# Lightface Celtic: ten-point source method

This family preserves the complete uppercase and lowercase of the labelled
ten-point Light-face Celtic cut, all ten figures, period, comma, semicolon and
hyphen. The empty word space is a modern layout advance. No visible character
is invented or borrowed from another size.

The Boston and Central 1889 specimen supplies an earlier named witness. ATF's
1896 collective specimen calls the design Lightface Celtic No. 40. The date
suffix identifies an inspected source, not the first release; the designer is
unidentified. The differently labelled ten-point Celtic types in the 1904
document specimen are separate designs.

Full native comparisons establish an optical distinction from the twelve-point
cut. In both 1897 and census witnesses, C is about 11–13 percent wider, M about
3–6 percent narrower, and j about 16–26 percent wider at a common cap reference.
The lowercase a/e/m bodies are lower relative to the capitals. Opposing changes
within one capital row cannot be explained by uniform page stretch. Three
threshold settings distinguish persistent proportions from impression variation.
`optical-comparison.json` retains measurements and exact native comparison crops;
the optical proof also compares the final ten- and twelve-point revivals.

Exact scans, locators, metadata and acquisition hashes are in `reference/`.
Census impressions supply A–O, a–o and all figures. The 1897 alphabet supplies
P–Z and q/x/z. Matching job-room text supplies p/r/s/t/u/v/w/y and punctuation.
Boston and ATF remain provenance and comparison witnesses. `selection.json`
identifies every selected impression; `source-inventories.json` records exact
source-local coverage across all five books.

The period follows “them” in the job-room market paragraph. It is a separately
observed component. The overlapping f/o earlier in that row is excluded from
the manual proposals; no invented cut separates joined impressions.

`tracing.json` reproduces the outlines with Potrace 1.16. Local illumination uses
radius 10 and a 0.35-pixel blur. Census threshold is 165, except b/g at 190. The
1897 alphabet uses 175, except q/x at 195. Job-room forms use 175. These settings
retain native thin joins and bowls that lower settings lose. Dots and semicolon
components remain separate. No holes are filled.

Capline and baseline regressions determine one uniform physical scale per source
page and a rigid rotation per row. The cap reference is 700 in a 1000-unit em;
lowercase retains its native proportions at the same page scale. Figures use a
locally measured baseline from 1/4/7 rather than extrapolation across page
curvature. No shear, independent width fitting or analytic stroke repair is used.

Modern sidebearings are 35 units and the word space is 300. Individual reviewed
limits in `sizing.json` preserve bounds through format conversion.
`source-quality.json` records source choices and rejected comparison settings.
